# Research and Sources

## Framework
The Three Cs (Compliment, Clarify, Contrast) is a response framework for
real estate agents handling "I already have an agent." Cameron supplied
a research and copy package as a starting brief, explicitly flagged in
that package as "not an approved design" and a prototype to verify and
refine, not final content.

## Attribution
The research package could not confirm an exact, verifiable primary
source for "Amir Syed" originating this specific Compliment / Clarify /
Contrast wording. I independently searched for confirmation and found
none either. Per both the package's own conclusion and this independent
check, the framework is presented on the flyer as an original Strategic
Home Loans working adaptation, with no name or attribution claimed
anywhere on either side.

## Legal/compliance research, independently verified
The single most compliance-sensitive claim in this piece is the
instruction to respect an existing exclusive buyer or listing
agreement before offering competing services. This was independently
verified directly against the National Association of Realtors (NAR)
Code of Ethics, not taken on the research package's word:

- **Source:** https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/governing-documents/code-of-ethics/the-code-of-ethics-article-16
- **Standard of Practice 16-9** (Amended 1/04): before entering a
  representation agreement, a REALTOR has an affirmative obligation to
  make reasonable efforts to determine whether a prospect is already
  bound by a current, valid exclusive agreement for the same type of
  service.
- **Standard of Practice 16-13** (Adopted 1/93, Amended 1/04): dealings
  concerning exclusively listed property, or with a buyer/tenant under
  an exclusive agreement, go through that party's representative or
  broker, not the client directly, except where that representative
  consents or the client initiates contact. Before providing
  substantive services (writing an offer, presenting a CMA), a REALTOR
  must ask whether the prospect is party to an exclusive agreement, and
  must not knowingly provide those services to someone under one unless
  their representative consents or they direct the work themselves.

This was checked twice, independently, across two separate work
sessions on this project, with the same result both times.

## Other sources the research package cited
These were reviewed but not independently re-verified word for word
(lower stakes than the Article 16 citation above, used for editorial
framing rather than quoted directly on the flyer):

- NAR consumer guide to written buyer agreements:
  https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-to-written-buyer-agreements
- NAR consumer guide to open houses and written agreements:
  https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-to-open-houses-and-written-agreements
- StreetEasy's agent-facing objection-handling guide (referenced for
  context on how "I already have an agent" gets used in practice, not
  quoted or copied): https://streeteasy.com/expert-resources/toolkit/scripts/responding-to-common-objections/
- Huang et al. (2017), "It Doesn't Hurt to Ask: Question-Asking
  Increases Liking" -- general supporting research on why a genuine
  clarifying question helps a conversation, not a claim about this
  specific framework's conversion rate: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-2eb6-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b

## What changed from the original research package
- The framework is not attributed to anyone by name, per the above.
- No invented statistics, no "proven to convert" language -- the
  research package itself flagged this as a risk and it was avoided.
- The original copy draft was rewritten in full for natural phrasing,
  the no-dash rule, and to fit the final visual concept -- substance
  preserved, wording is not copied verbatim from the package.
- The compliance guidance was tightened into short, bolded, scannable
  lines after an independent creative review found the original dense
  paragraph form was the hardest part of the page to actually use in a
  live conversation, which defeats the point of a compliance reminder.
