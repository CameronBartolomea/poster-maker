## Patterns that recur across all eight and matter most for the Three Cs

1. **The number or the phrase IS the graphic.** None of these illustrate a metaphor standing in for the content; the content itself (a rate, a percentage, a quoted line) is rendered at dominant scale. Nothing here needed a drawing of an unrelated object to "represent" an idea.
2. **One dominant focal point, everything else secondary.** Every example has exactly one thing the eye lands on first (a number, a wheel, a table), never 2-3 equally weighted elements competing.
3. **Real content density, patterned so it scans.** The rate tables and the 5-wedge wheel and the 5-row equity list are not sparse — they carry real information — but a strict repeating row/wedge pattern lets the eye skip through them in seconds.
4. **Translation devices turn abstraction into something concrete** (Amazon habit -> $30-35K -> "3-car garage, backyard pool"). The abstraction is never left to a symbol; it's spelled out in named, specific terms.
5. **A wheel or flow is used when the content is genuinely structural** (5 real benefits of one role) — not as a substitute for having real content, but as the right-shaped container for content that actually has that shape.
6. **Color is restrained to 1-2 accents against a neutral base**, used consistently (green=savings, red=cost, one brand hue per variant), never applied just to fill space.

## Where this points for the Three Cs

The Three Cs front needs a graphic whose *shape* is doing real work,
not standing in for the content. Three credible directions, matched to
what's actually in these references:
- A **numbered-step list with icon chips and exact quoted speech**
  (closest to "Increase Your Home Equity") — the quoted lines
  themselves are the content, rendered large, the way these
  references render a rate or a percentage large.
- A **decision pathway / flowchart with a represented-client exit**
  (a genuinely different structural device than anything in the
  references, but the same spirit as the Buyer Agent wheel: a real
  diagram whose shape encodes the actual decision logic, not a
  metaphor).
- An **editorial, quote-led composition** where the objection and the
  three responses are rendered at the oversized, confident type scale
  the "6.875% -> 4.875%" and "71%" references use, with color doing the
  same restrained signaling job it does throughout the reference set.
