For the Agent, Not the Client

What to say when someone already has an agent

A short way to keep the conversation open without stepping on anyone's relationship. Three moves, in order, and a reminder on where the line is.

01 · Compliment
“That's great, having someone you trust matters.”

Costs nothing. Say it and mean it before you ask anything else.

02 · Clarify
“Are you working together on a signed agreement, or is that someone you've used before?”

This is the question that actually matters. It tells you whether the door is open at all.

03 · Contrast
“What matters most to you in how an agent works with you?”

Only if the door is open. Point to one specific, true difference. Keep it short.

One Way This Actually Sounds
Agent

Hi, thanks for stopping by. Are you working with an agent already?

Prospect

Yeah, we've got someone.

Agent

That's great, glad you've got someone in your corner. Are you two working together officially right now, or is it more that you've used them before?

Prospect

We used her to buy our last place. Haven't signed anything for this one.

Agent

Got it. Mind if I ask what you're hoping goes differently this time?

Prospect

Honestly, communication. We felt out of the loop a lot.

Agent

That's really common. If it would help, I can show you how I keep people updated week to week. Might be useful even if you end up going back to her.

Respect the Relationship

If someone is currently under a signed exclusive agreement for this transaction, that relationship comes first. Don't offer competing services, don't go around their agent, and don't treat Contrast as a way to work around it. Per NAR's Code of Ethics (Article 16, Standards of Practice 16-9 and 16-13), ask about an existing agreement before providing substantive services, and route anything involving an exclusively represented party through their agent, unless that agent agrees otherwise or the prospect reaches out to you directly. When in doubt, follow your brokerage's current policy.

Same Idea, Other Objections
"We're not ready yet."
"Understood. What would need to change before it felt right to move forward?"
"We're just looking."
"Of course. What are you hoping to learn while you look?"
"We're waiting on the market."
"That makes sense. What would you want to see happen before the timing felt right?"
What to Avoid

Criticizing the other agent. Pressuring for a commitment. Assuming someone is unrepresented just because they did not mention an agreement. Promising a result you cannot guarantee.

For real estate agent education. Follow your brokerage's current policies and the representation requirements that apply to you. See NAR Code of Ethics, Article 16, Standards of Practice 16-9 and 16-13.
